# Testing with children

Computerfuchs is made for children, and only children can show whether it
works. This guide helps parents and teachers run a short, relaxed test and give
useful feedback. No technical knowledge is needed.

## In short

- **Keep it short:** 15 minutes with five-year-olds, 20 to 30 minutes with older
  children.
- **The child plays, you watch.** Don't help straight away – count to ten first.
  The places where a child gets stuck are the most valuable ones.
- **Let them think aloud:** "Tell me what you're doing."
- **Write down** where the child hesitates, is amazed, laughs or wants to give up.
- **Three questions** at the end, then [send feedback](#sending-feedback).

## Preparation

- **Device:** a laptop or PC **with a mouse**. Use a touchpad only if the child
  already knows it – it is very hard for small children. Speakers on.
- **Browser:** Chrome or Edge (they have the nicest read-aloud voices), otherwise
  Firefox. The address is
  [technikweber.github.io/Computerfuchs](https://technikweber.github.io/Computerfuchs/),
  or use a [kiosk](KIOSK.md). The app is in German.
- **A new profile:** the child creates it – that is part of the test. For a
  second test, delete the profile in the grown-ups' area (Einstellungen → Für
  Erwachsene).
- **A nickname instead of the real name** helps if you want to share screenshots
  or the save file later.
- **Pen and paper**, ideally with the [observation sheet](#observation-sheet)
  below.
- **Ask whether the child wants to take part.** They may stop at any time. Please
  don't publish photos or videos of the child.

## Plan by age

The stations are suggestions. If a child enjoys something, let them stay there –
that is an important observation too.

### Ages 5 and 6 (about 15 minutes)

1. **Switch on:** does the child find the power button? Please don't point.
2. **Create a profile:** pick an animal and a name (help with typing is fine),
   then the age.
3. **Mouse course**, levels 1 to 4: Taschenlampe (flashlight), Ballons, Geschenke
   (double-click), Tiere füttern (dragging).
4. **Keyboard:** Tastenzoo and Hüpfhase, tier 1 each.
5. **App store:** are there enough Bytes for a sticker yet? Stick it on the
   desktop.

Watch especially: does the child understand the spoken instructions? How long
does it take until the double-click works? Do they get impatient?

### Ages 7 to 9 (20 to 30 minutes)

1. **Create a profile.**
2. **Mouse course**, levels 3 to 6: Geschenke, Tiere füttern, Bienenweg,
   Farbenzauber (right-click).
3. **Maths:** plus to 20.
4. **Keyboard:** Bilderwörter and Sternenregen.
5. **Files:** Flinn's tasks 1 to 4.
6. **App store:** get a wallpaper and set it from the "Downloads" folder.

### Ages 10 and up (20 to 30 minutes)

1. **Create a profile**, set a secret code under "Ich", sign out and back in.
2. **Files:** all six tasks, plus copying a picture to another folder with Ctrl+C
   and Ctrl+V.
3. **Keyboard:** Zehn Finger and Tipp-Profi up to the race against Flinn.
4. **Maths:** times tables or mental arithmetic.
5. **Explore freely:** "Have a look around – what can you find?"

## What to watch for

- Where does the child **hesitate** for more than ten seconds?
- Where do they **click** next to things, or on something that isn't clickable?
- Do they understand what **Flinn** says? Do they read, or listen?
- What is **fun** (laughter, "again!")?
- Where do they get **frustrated** or give up?
- Is something **too easy** (boring) or **too hard**?
- Do they notice they earn **Bytes**? Do they want to spend them?
- **Technical:** does anything stutter, is sound missing, is something cut off?

## Three questions at the end

1. "What was the most fun?"
2. "What was annoying or hard?"
3. "What would you like to learn on the computer next?"

And for you: would you let your child practise with it again? What was missing?

## Sending feedback

The easiest way is GitHub: open
[github.com/TechnikWeber/Computerfuchs/issues](https://github.com/TechnikWeber/Computerfuchs/issues),
click **"New issue"** and paste your notes. One issue per test is enough.

Most helpful:

- the child's **age** (not the name)
- **device and browser**, mouse or touchpad
- **what worked** and **where they got stuck**, ideally with level and tier –
  for example "Mouse, level 3 Geschenke, tier 2: double-click too fast"
- **screenshots** are welcome, but without the child's real name
- if you like, the **save file** (Einstellungen → Für Erwachsene → "Alle Profile
  als Datei sichern"). It shows which levels were played how often. It only
  contains what was entered in Computerfuchs: profile name, animal, age, progress
  and the files from the files app. Change the name first if the child used
  their real one.

Computerfuchs itself sends nothing anywhere – there is no analytics and no
server listening. What you share is entirely up to you.

## At school or in a group

- Two children at one device explain a lot to each other – often more revealing
  than any interview.
- Each device saves on its own. Collect save files only with the parents'
  consent.
- For a room with several computers, a [kiosk](KIOSK.md) is worth it, so nobody
  ends up somewhere else by accident.
